[center]**Achieving Success In Telepathy**[/center]


Telepathy is the transfer of information from one mind to another. Reports of telepathic experiences appear throughout history and across many cultures. Many believe that every human being has some telepathic skill. Genetics appears to play a role, since native ESP ability has been observed to run in families. If everyone has at least some ability, it seems likely that anyone who works seriously at improving telepathic skill will experience increasing success over time.

The training system in ESP Gym was developed to optimize the conditions under which telepathic ability can be practiced and strengthened. Performance is monitored using established statistical methods. The practice tool uses a synchronized countdown that both sender and receiver share in real time, wherever they are in the world: Three... two... one... appers on each person’s screen, and an image is presented to the sender. Detecting telepathic information is easier when the receiver knows exactly when the sender begins attending to the image.

When the countdown ends on the receiver's screen, the receiver’s screen goes blank and a short beep sounds. The receiver then typically has their eyes closed and their mind quiet and notices what appears surprising in the contents of their mind's eye. What newly appears there, as if from the outside, at about the time the countdown ends? This is the extra sensory perceptual task the receiver endeavors to develop.

When visual information is perceived in the mind’s eye at that time, it is usually low in contrast, unclear, and fleeting. Sometimes color is obviously present. A few visual features can often be picked out, along with their relative size and position, for example on the left or the right. Even a small amount of accurate visual feature information can be enough to distinguish the target image, the image viewed by the sender, from one or more “decoy images” that are shown to the receiver with different visual features. In Level 1 of the practice tests, all that has to be perceieved is whether there is one cone or more than one cone (three cones).

The receiver must do more than notice features that appear fleetingly in their mind's eye. The receiver must also remember one or more shapes, their color and their location. This is not simple when looking at the disconnected forms that are perceived. Much of this often happens within the first few seconds after the countdown ends. This is the moment when the visual information is new for the sender. Brainwave research has shown that new visual information tends to produce a strong transient burst of brain activity in the person looking at it. Because the image is new for the sender, it would also be new for the telepathic receiver at that time. This is a good time to notice it, since when information generates a stronger response in the sender it also tends to be received more strongly.

When we think about telepathy logically, we are tempted to conclude that it must be impossible. How can any signal not degrade with distance? Yet repeated experiments suggest that telepathic information is not measurably diminished by distance or by intervening obstacles, including mountains, oceans, metal shielding, and the earth itself. How can something as subtle as a thought in one person's mind be conveyed across a few feet or thousands of miles with no change in apparent strength? And how can one person tune in effortlessly to the thoughts of a single other person anywhere on earth while billions of other minds are active at the same time? Yet this exactly what happens. The connection between the correct sender and the receiver is established unconsciously, automatically, and without effort.

It is important to realize that the fact that something cannot yet be explained by current science does not mean that it cannot exist. Belief is stubborn. For example, it took many years for intelligent physicians to adopt handwashing before delivering babies. Many resisted because they could not accept the idea that invisible germs were real and could carry disease, even after Ignaz Semmelweis showed that hand washing greatly reduced sickness and death in mothers after childbirth.

A receiver needs to fully believe that telepathy exists and fully trust that the information is available in their mind's eye after the countdown. It has been shown that those who don't believe that telepathy exists are much less likely to experience it. However, it has also been shown that a sender does not need to believe in telepathy in order to effectively send telepathic information.

A receiver has greater success when their state of mind is calm rather than agitated while attempting to receive information telepathically. There are many reports of people receiving such information in dreams. If a receiver is overly concerned about getting good results, or experiences performance anxiety, this can significantly reduce the ability to receive effectively. A more playful or detached attitude is better. The less anxiety one brings into the task, the better one is often able to perform. This type of relaxed "don't care" attitude takes time to develop. With repeated familiarity, the experience of receiving telepathic information becomes less startling and less unusual, and more like just another successful act of ESP.

A final thought: many people, when first considering telepathy, are willing to believe that it may work for some others, yet still feel strongly that they themselves do not have the sensitivity needed to receive telepathic information. They may think they could serve as a sender, but not as a successful receiver. Yet when some of these same people actually give receiving a try, they are surprised to discover that they really can be telepathic. The advice to those who assume they are incapable of receiving is simple: get over it.

Mizu no kokoro      

“This term was emphasized in the teachings of the ancient karate masters. It refers to the mental attitude required while facing an actual opponent. Mizu no kokoro (Mind like water) refers to the need to make the mind calm, like the surface of undisturbed water. To carry the symbolism further, smooth water reflects accurately image of all objects within its range, and if the mind is kept in this state, apprehension of the opponent’s movements, both psychological and physical, will be both immediate and accurate, and one’s responses, both defensive and offensive, will be appropriate and adequate. On the other hand, if the surface of the water is disturbed, the images it reflects will be distorted, or by analogy, if the mind is preoccupied with thoughts of attack or defense, it will not apprehend the opponent’s intentions, creating an opportunity for the opponent to attack.”
                                   -Sensei H. Nishiyama
