[center]THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CLAIRVOYANCE AND REMOTE VIEWING[/center]

Clairvoyance and remote viewing are closely related, but they are not exactly the same thing. "Clairvoyance" means perceiving a hidden, distant, or otherwise inaccessible target directly, without the ordinary senses. "Remote viewing" is usually understood as a more structured, disciplined, and testable form of hidden target perception, often carried out under blind, controlled conditions, and followed by judging. Clairvoyance is the broader category, while remote viewing is a special method or application within that broader category.

If a picture is displayed on a monitor screen that you cannot see because it is covered, the cleanest term for this ESP is *clairvoyance.* More specifically, one might call it *screened-target clairvoyance.*

Why This Distinction Matters

In clairvoyance, the question is whether the person can directly perceive a hidden target. In remote viewing, the question is again whether hidden-target perception occurs, but now the task is arranged in a way that gives the viewer more time to perceive the target and record perceptions related to the target. The complete response is preserved. This makes it easier to test fairly and evaluate it afterward. The response is not simply one out of two choices, but partial information can be used to see what features may have been received correctly, even in the case of an incorrect choice.

Remote viewing is better understood as a more formal way of doing clairvoyant target perception.


What Clairvoyant Perception Usually Feels Like

As with telepathy, the impressions associated with clairvoyance or remote viewing are often subtle, vague, partial, and short-lived. Don't expect a clear internal photograph of the target. More often, what appears is fragmentary visual information: bits of shape, color, texture, motion, contrast, or spatial arrangement. The information may come as fleeting impressions rather than as a stable image.

That is why it helps to quiet the mind, reduce internal chatter, and observe carefully and remember any surprising visual information that appeared spontaneously and was perceived.


A Simple Remote-Viewing-Style Protocol

If the target image is hidden from the user and the user simply tries to perceive it and then chooses between two images, this is best thought of as a form of clairvoyance practice.

If the user wants to make the session lean more toward remote viewing, the procedure can be made more formal. For example, the user can leave the covered or remote image on the screen for a longer period of time and use a sketchbook to draw impressions. Instead of trying only for a quick recognition, the user can allow more time for subtle impressions to develop and be recorded.

After that, the user can continue the trial in the usual way and indicate which of the two images shown appears to best match the impressions.

Then, after one or more trials, the user can go to the Performance Reports Raw Data for those trials and show a judge or judges:

- the sketchbook impressions
- the actual target image
- the decoy image

The judge can then rate which of the two images better matches the sketch. This makes the procedure more like remote viewing because it introduces preserved free-response material and later judging.

A hidden-screen ESP GYM Level-4 experiment approximates a remote viewing experiment when:

1. The target is selected randomly from a well-prepared pool of clearly different images. (ESP GYM does this.)

2. The viewer does not know the target. (Supported by ESP GYM.)

3. No one present with the viewer knows the target either.

4. The viewer has ample time to observe impressions, talk about them into a recorder, or sketch impressions. This is possible by simply waiting to bring up the final two-image choice screen in until ready.

5. The response is preserved exactly, for example in an audio recording and/or notes and/or sketches.

6. Afterward, the response is compared with the true target and one or more decoys. (ESP GYM provides one decoy image.)

7. A judge or judges evaluate which image best matches the recorded impressions. (If no judge is available, you can be the judge to see how will your recorded impressions match the target.) In order to judge, go to the RAW DATA report for the condition you just worked in, where the images on each trial are shown and can be enlarged. 

8. Results are analyzed over repeated trials.

These features help reduce guessing and wishful interpretation. They also make the experiment more scientifically meaningful.



Why Sketching Can Be Valuable

Sketching is important because ESP impressions are often difficult to put into words. A person may perceive a curve, a cluster, a contrast, a vertical form, or a texture without knowing what object it belongs to.

A sketch can preserve these partial impressions better than language alone. Later, a judge may see meaningful correspondences between the sketch and the target even when the viewer could not name the target consciously.

Sketching can make the process both more informative and more testable.


Conclusion

The most accurate way to think about this is:

- If you are trying to perceive a hidden image directly, that is fundamentally clairvoyance.

- If you arrange the task in a blind, structured, record-preserving, and judgeable way, it becomes remote-viewing-style clairvoyance.

In ESP GYM, the same basic tool can be used as simple clairvoyance practice, or it can be made more like remote viewing by allowing longer observation time, recording impressions carefully, and using later judging against target and decoy images.
